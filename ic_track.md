# Individual Contributor Track

Six levels. Each one is described against the same four dimensions: scope, execution, influence, and operational ownership. The one sentence summary is the test I actually use: if you cannot honestly say that sentence about someone, they are not at that level yet.

A note on time: there are no minimum years here. I have seen people reach Senior in three years and I have seen people plateau happily there for fifteen. Both are fine. Levels describe how someone operates, not how long they have survived.

---

## E1: Engineer

**In one sentence:** delivers well defined tasks with guidance, and is learning the team's systems and standards.

- **Scope:** individual tasks, scoped and reviewed by others
- **Execution:** writes working, tested code; uses AI tools as a matter of course and can tell when the output is wrong before it ships; asks for help before getting stuck for days
- **Influence:** participates in code review and team discussion; learning to give useful feedback
- **Operations:** shadows on call; knows how to find runbooks and escalate

## E2: Engineer II

**In one sentence:** independently delivers features end to end inside the team's domain.

- **Scope:** features and small projects, owned start to finish
- **Execution:** breaks work down sensibly; anticipates edge cases; their changes rarely surprise anyone in production
- **Influence:** reliable code reviewer; mentors interns and new hires on the basics
- **Operations:** takes on call rotations; resolves routine incidents without help

## E3: Senior Engineer

**In one sentence:** owns a system or service, including its technical direction, and makes the people around them better.

- **Scope:** a system or service area, including its roadmap of technical work
- **Execution:** designs before building; their designs survive contact with production; knows when not to build
- **Influence:** the person others come to with hard questions in their area; raises the team's bar, including how the team uses AI, through review and example
- **Operations:** leads incident response in their area; writes the postmortems others learn from

This is a terminal level. A career spent operating well at Senior is a successful career, full stop.

## E4: Staff Engineer

**In one sentence:** solves problems that span teams, and the org's plans change based on their technical judgment.

- **Scope:** problems crossing two or more teams; ambiguity is the default
- **Execution:** picks the right problems, not just executes assigned ones; ships through other people's hands as often as their own
- **Influence:** trusted by managers and directors as a technical advisor; disagreements with them get taken seriously
- **Operations:** designs the systems and practices that prevent whole classes of incidents

## E5: Senior Staff Engineer

**In one sentence:** owns the technical health of an entire domain and trades it off against business priorities credibly.

- **Scope:** a domain (e.g. all of platform, all of data); horizon measured in years
- **Execution:** the architecture in their domain is intentional rather than accidental, and they can explain why to an executive or a new hire
- **Influence:** shapes engineering wide standards, including how the domain adopts AI; develops Staff engineers deliberately
- **Operations:** accountable for reliability outcomes in the domain, not just response quality

## E6: Principal Engineer

**In one sentence:** their technical judgment shapes company strategy, and they are a reason strong engineers join and stay.

- **Scope:** the company's technical direction; works across every domain
- **Execution:** finds the small number of technical bets that matter and makes them win
- **Influence:** peer to directors and VPs; represents the company's engineering externally
- **Operations:** owns the standards by which the org judges operational excellence

---

## How to read the gaps between levels

The jump from E2 to E3 is mastery of a known space. The jump from E3 to E4 is the hard one: it is a change of kind, not degree. Past E3, the question stops being "how good is your work" and becomes "how much better is the organization because you are in it." Plenty of brilliant builders never make that shift and they should not be forced to. That is what terminal levels are for.
