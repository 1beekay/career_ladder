# Engineering Career Ladder

A practical career ladder for engineering organizations, from new engineer to VP. I built and rolled out a version of this at a growth stage SaaS company while scaling the engineering org from 15 to 40+ people, and refined the thinking across orgs of up to 80. This repo is the genericized version: take it, adapt it, argue with it.

## Why bother with a ladder

Without a written ladder, careers are negotiated instead of earned. The loudest people get promoted, managers improvise expectations, and every comp conversation starts from zero. A good ladder fixes that by making three things explicit:

1. What each level looks like in observable behavior, not years served
2. That promotion is recognition of a level you already operate at, not a bet on potential
3. That the senior IC track is a real destination, not a waiting room for management

## Design principles

**Few dimensions, applied consistently.** Every level is described against the same four dimensions: scope, execution, influence, and operational ownership. If you need ten dimensions, your levels are doing too much work.

**Scope is the spine.** The clearest signal of level is the size of the problem someone can own without supervision: a task, a feature, a system, a domain, an organization. Everything else hangs off that.

**Behaviors, not adjectives.** "Shows strong leadership" is useless in a calibration meeting. "Other teams change their plans based on this person's technical direction" is something you can check.

**Both tracks top out at equivalent levels.** A Principal Engineer and a Director sit at the same altitude, are paid in the same band, and neither reports to the other by default. The moment management is the only path to seniority, you start losing your best engineers to other companies or, worse, to management jobs they do not want.
