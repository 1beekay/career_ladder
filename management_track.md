# Management Track

Five levels, same four dimensions as the IC track: scope, execution, influence, and operational ownership. The execution dimension changes meaning here: managers deliver through teams, and the output being judged is the team's output plus the health of the system that produced it.

The most important rule on this track: management is a career change, not a promotion. An E3 or E4 moving into management starts a new craft from the beginning. Make that explicit, make it reversible, and you will get better managers and keep more engineers.

---

## M1: Engineering Manager

**In one sentence:** runs one healthy team that ships, and every person on it knows where they stand.

- **Scope:** one team, typically 5 to 8 engineers
- **Execution:** the team delivers predictably; commitments mean something; technical debt is managed, not ignored
- **People:** gives honest feedback early; runs real one on ones; nobody on the team is surprised by their performance review
- **Operations:** owns the team's on call health and incident quality; pushes back when reliability is being traded away silently

## M2: Senior Engineering Manager

**In one sentence:** runs multiple teams or a large complex one, and grows the managers and senior engineers inside it.

- **Scope:** two to three teams, or one team with an outsized blast radius
- **Execution:** balances roadmaps across teams; finds the staffing and sequencing moves that make both teams faster
- **People:** develops first time managers and Staff candidates deliberately; calibrates fairly across teams
- **Operations:** accountable for reliability across the group; runs the postmortem culture, not just the postmortems

## M3: Director of Engineering

**In one sentence:** owns an organization and its results, designs its structure, and represents it to the rest of the company.

- **Scope:** an org of multiple teams, typically 15 to 40+ engineers, including managers of managers at the high end
- **Execution:** owns budget, headcount, and the org design itself; restructures when the org shape stops matching the problem
- **People:** hires and grows managers; builds the bench so no single departure is a crisis; owns culture deliberately
- **Operations:** sets reliability and operational standards for the org; answers for them to executives, customers, and auditors

## M4: Senior Director of Engineering

**In one sentence:** runs a business critical slice of engineering and operates as a peer to the executive team on strategy.

- **Scope:** a major engineering pillar; often 40 to 80+ engineers across functions and geographies
- **Execution:** turns company strategy into multi year engineering investment; kills projects that no longer earn their cost
- **People:** develops Directors; succession plans exist and are real
- **Operations:** owns operational outcomes at the level the board hears about: uptime, security posture, cost of infrastructure

## M5: VP of Engineering

**In one sentence:** owns engineering as a function of the business, and the company's results are partly their results.

- **Scope:** all of engineering, or all of a product line's engineering in a large company
- **Execution:** the engineering operating model is theirs: how work is planned, shipped, measured, and paid for
- **People:** builds the leadership team; the company's engineering reputation in the market is a thing they manage
- **Operations:** reliability, security, and infrastructure cost are business commitments they make and keep

---

## What does not change as you go up

At every level the job is the same three loops: set direction, build the team that can execute it, and inspect the results honestly. What changes is the time horizon and how indirect your hands are. An EM corrects course weekly with people they talk to every day. A VP corrects course quarterly through managers of managers, using systems they had to design themselves. If you cannot get results without being in every room, you are capped at M2 no matter what the title says.
